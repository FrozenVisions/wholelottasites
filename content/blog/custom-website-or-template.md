A custom website or template is a choice about who builds the pages. A template, or a DIY builder, suits you if you have the evenings and you accept a shared layout. A custom site is written for what you do, where you work, and how a customer gets in touch. I’m Chris Faulkes, a web designer based in Mansfield, Nottinghamshire, working with small businesses in the East Midlands. I won’t guess another company’s price.

## What a custom website or template is asking

People search this when cards, a Facebook page or an old theme is no longer enough. Ask which version a customer can use on a phone, and which you will still update in six months.

A template starts as someone else’s layout, which you fill in. A custom site is built around the work you take: the pages, their order, and how someone calls. Here that is [a website built from scratch](/website-built-from-scratch/), at the same small-business price, not a higher tier.

## Template website vs custom website

### What a template does well

A template is a fair way to get online when three things are true.

- You have the hours to learn the builder and finish the pages.
- What you do, and where, is simple to write.
- A shared layout does not bother you.

You can change a price yourself, and you should keep a copy of the words and photos.

### Where a template gets in the way

- Unused sections stay on the page and add weight.
- On a phone, the text needs a pinch, or the number will not dial.
- The title still says “Home”. Each page needs its own title and a short description. That is not a promise of a Google position.
- A price list in text, the towns you cover, or a form that reaches you may fight the theme.

A builder only helps if you finish it. One fault on a live site in Nottinghamshire, Derbyshire, Leicestershire, Lincolnshire or Northamptonshire can be the [website mini-fix](/website-mini-fix/): £50 + VAT (£60 inc VAT) for a short audit and one agreed fix. A wider fix is usually £150–£400 after a free audit. If a patch will not hold, I’ll say so before you pay.

## Website builder or web designer

Stay with a website builder if you enjoy it and will keep the hours and prices current. Ask a web designer if the tool is delaying the launch. A trade needs the towns and a number that dials. A shop needs the hours and a way to ask. I build that page. I don’t do the trade.

### A checklist before you pay anyone

On a phone, or on a blank note if you have no site yet, answer these in order.

1. Can a stranger say what you do, where, and how to reach you, without a menu?
2. Does the number dial when tapped, and does a test form arrive with you?
3. Can you read a price, a menu or the address without pinching to zoom?
4. Do you have the hours to build the pages, and to change them when a price moves?
5. Are you content with a layout many other businesses on that theme already use?
6. Is the domain in your name, with the words and photos kept outside the builder?
7. If a site is already live, is this one fault, a wider repair, or a replacement?

If the first three fail and you will not fix them yourself, the template is not finished. If you have the hours and a shared layout does not bother you, stay with the builder. If the domain is not yours, sort that before you pay anyone, including me.
