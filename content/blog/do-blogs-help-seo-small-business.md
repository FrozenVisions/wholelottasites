Do blogs help SEO for small businesses? They can. A clear post is another page about a question a customer types into Google: a local service phrase, a “how much”, or a “do I need”. It builds slowly, over months. I’m Chris Faulkes, a web designer in Mansfield, Nottinghamshire. Nothing here is guaranteed. There is no promise of a Google position, of rankings, or of leads.

## Do blogs help SEO for small businesses?

They can, for the questions a short homepage never quite covers. [What a small business website needs](/blog/what-a-small-business-website-needs/) is the short list: what you do, where you work, and how to get in touch. A blog picks up the search someone types while they are still deciding who to ring.

### Local service phrases

A local service phrase is the work plus the place, in the words a customer would use. “Boiler repair in Mansfield.” “Emergency plumber in Nottingham.” “Café near the market.” The post names the job, the towns you actually cover, and how to get in touch. It should match the homepage and your [Google Business Profile](/google-business-profile/): the same name, the same phone number, the same area. If they disagree, the customer and any software reading the page both have to guess.

### “How much” and “do I need”

Say what a job depends on: the size of the room, how far you travel, what a visit includes. You do not have to print a figure you cannot keep. Tell the same truth you already tell on the phone. “Do I need” questions work the same way. Do I need a website if I already have a Facebook page? Do I need a full replacement, or a repair? A straight answer includes the times the answer is no. If you would not say the sentence out loud, leave it out.

### A page Google and an assistant can read

Each post is its own address, with a title, a short description and real text. That is what Google can read, and what a tool such as ChatGPT can read. Put the answer in sentences, not only inside a picture or a logo. [Whether ChatGPT will recommend a business](/blog/will-chatgpt-recommend-my-business/) is a separate note. Nobody can promise a mention. Link each post to the service page the reader should open next.

## It builds over months

One post this week will not show you a change this week. Keep adding a clear answer to a different question, and leave the pages up. Think months. I won’t invent a number of visits or a result. Nothing is guaranteed. There is no promise of a Google position, of rankings, or of leads.

A one-off thin post, written only to hold a search phrase, is not worth publishing. Keyword stuffing, the same phrase forced into every line, does not make the page clearer. Use the customer’s words where the sentence needs them, then answer the question. If you will not keep going, skip the empty blog. The page has to be about your work and your area. I won’t invent a client, a review or a result to fill a gap.

## Before you start a blog

1. Can you name the questions customers ask before they call, in their words?
2. Will each post answer one of those, and name the towns you really cover?
3. Do the phone number, the business name and the area match the website and the Google listing?
4. Will you keep publishing for months, knowing nothing is guaranteed?
5. If any answer is no, sort the main pages first.
