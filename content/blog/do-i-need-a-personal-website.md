“Do I need a personal website?” is what people type when someone has their name and nowhere useful to send them. You need one when they look you up and the page they land on is not yours. LinkedIn, Instagram or a mention on someone else’s site can still help. None of them is an address you control. If nobody is looking you up, and you do not want them to, you can wait.

I’m Chris Faulkes, a web designer in Mansfield. I build small sites for businesses and for people who are not a company. What I would actually make is on [personal websites](/websites-for/individuals/).

## When the answer is yes

The page earns its place when what comes up for your name is an old employer, a namesake, or a profile that does not say what you do now. You also want one if you need a single link, under your own name, for a bio, a card or an email. You want one if a side project’s only public home is a Facebook group, or if you keep sending people a profile that hides how to reach you.

The domain is the address, often your own name, registered in your name rather than a designer’s. Search your name before you buy one. If a namesake has the obvious domain, add a middle initial or the thing you do.

## When you can leave it

A personal website is not a requirement of being online. If you do not want strangers to find you, do not publish a page that helps them. If a company site already says who you are and how to get in touch, a second site in your own name usually repeats it.

A dating profile is a different job. So is a shop with live stock and shipping. If you are about to trade under a business name, start there: see [a first website for a new business](/blog/first-website-for-my-business/), and the pages for [freelancers](/websites-for/freelancers/) and [consultants](/websites-for/consultants/). A consultant who wants the longer list can read [what to put on a consultant’s website](/blog/consultant-website-pages/). The wider list is [websites for small businesses](/websites-for/).

## What to put on it

One page is often enough, and it is shorter than [what a small business website needs](/blog/what-a-small-business-website-needs/).

1. **Your name, in the title and the first line.** That is what they typed. “Home” is a weak title.
2. **One paragraph in your own words.** I will not add awards, follower counts, job titles or years of experience you did not give me.
3. **Work you can show, or a gap.** Only publish work you are allowed to show. If you cannot name a past employer’s project, describe the kind of work and leave the name off.
4. **Contact on your terms.** A form, an email you read, or both. Leave the phone number off if you do not want calls, and leave your home address off unless you want strangers to have it. A number inside a logo cannot be dialled.

Read it on a phone before you send the link. I can’t promise where it will appear on Google.
