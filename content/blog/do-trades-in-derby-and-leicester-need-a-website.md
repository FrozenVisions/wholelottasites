## What people actually do before they call

Someone with a leak, a dead socket or a wall that needs skimming does not start by searching “website for trades”. They search the problem, or “plumber Derby”, “electrician Leicester”, “roofer near me”. They want three facts quickly: you cover their area, you do that job, and they can phone you without pinching the screen.

A social profile can show photos. It often hides the phone number, changes its layout, and disappears if the account is limited. A marketplace listing is someone else’s page, with someone else’s rules. A website at a domain you own is the place those other profiles should point to. Your Google Business Profile should use the same name, address and phone number as that site, word for word. When they disagree, the customer does not know which to trust. I still will not promise that the listing or the site will sit at the top of Google.

## What a trade site needs, and what it doesn’t

It needs the services named separately. Boiler repair is not the same enquiry as a bathroom. Rewires are not the same as extra sockets. A gallery helps when the work is something people want to see, and it should be your work, not stock photos of a van that isn’t yours.

It needs the towns, written out. Derby, Spondon, Allestree, Leicester, Wigston, Oadby: whichever you actually cover. A map pin is not a substitute for the words. It needs a tappable phone number and a form that reaches you. It needs to be readable on a phone without zooming, because that is the device in their hand.

It does not need twenty pages that repeat one sentence. It does not need a blog you will never write. It does not need a booking system built into the site if you already use one and can link to it. It does not need invented reviews, star ratings or a line about being the best in the county. I won’t write those, and a site that has them when they are not true is a liability.
