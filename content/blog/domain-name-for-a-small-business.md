A domain name for a small business is the address on the van, the quote and the Google listing. That address is what customers learn. I’m Chris Faulkes, a web designer in Mansfield. This is how I choose one with a UK owner. The registration stays in your name.

## The address, and the website

The domain is the name in the browser, such as yourbusiness.co.uk. The website is the pages at that name. A Facebook page, an Instagram account or a free subdomain is someone else’s address. Register a domain you control and point those profiles at it. What to publish is in [your first website for a new business](/blog/first-website-for-my-business/).

## The name on the quote

Use the trading name, not a slogan and not a list of every town. It should be recognisable as the sign. A short form is fine once you have checked it is not another firm. Include a town when it is part of the trading name. If you cover several towns, write them on the site.

Match the pages to the work: [plumbers](/websites-for/plumbers/), [electricians](/websites-for/electricians/), [builders](/websites-for/builders/), or the full list at [websites for small businesses](/websites-for/).

Say it aloud. If a hyphen, a number or an extra plural needs explaining, pick another name. Use that spelling on the site and the email. Local keywords belong in the page text. That is not a promise of a Google position.

## The ending

For a UK firm, .co.uk is the ending people expect to type. .uk is fine when that is what you will say. Consider .com if you sell abroad, or if another firm already holds the .co.uk and customers could mix you up. .org.uk suits a club, a charity or a community group more than a shop or a trade. Skip an ending you have to spell every time.

Buy the one you will print. A further name is not included. Premium domains cost extra. I will not invent a renewal price.

Search first, and leave a nearby firm’s name, a well-known brand, or a scheme you are not in. I am not a solicitor.

## Your name on the registration

You, or the company, should be the registrant, and you should be able to log in. I don’t need to own the domain to build the site. If a previous company holds it, ask for the transfer while they still reply. Note the registrar, the account email and the renewal month. If it lapses, someone else can buy it.

I help point it at the new site. The account stays yours. For a .uk name the registry is Nominet, and you still buy through a registrar. Keep the account email current so the reminder arrives. If you work from home, ask the registrar what address is published before you type the street in.

## Email, and the same details everywhere

Forward info@yourbusiness.co.uk to an inbox you already read. A separate mailbox is a different product. On a founding-client website, that forwarding is included. It is not a hosted mailbox.

Use the same name, phone number and web address on the site and on your [Google Business Profile](/google-business-profile/). Setup or a fix is £149 on its own, and included with a founding website. You stay the owner. Optional management is £99–£150 a month. I can’t promise where the listing appears, and I won’t write the reviews.

## A name you have already printed

Keep the domain on the van unless nobody can spell it, or the holder will not transfer it.

## Decide it this week

1. Write the trading name as it appears on a quote.
2. Say the address aloud. Change it if the spelling needs explaining.
3. Search the plural, the hyphen, and any similar local name.
4. Prefer .co.uk unless another ending is the one you will say.
5. Register it in your name and keep the login.
6. Forward one email address to an inbox you read.
7. Use that address on the site, the listing, the van and the quotes.
