## What to line up on a Google Business Profile and website

Open the listing and the site on the same phone. Read them as a customer would.

1. **The business name.** Use the same name in both places. A shortened name, an old trading name, or extra words added only on the listing make the two look like different businesses.
2. **The phone number.** One number, written as real text on the site, so a phone can dial it. If the only number sits inside a picture of a van, the listing and the site have already drifted apart.
3. **The address, or the area you cover.** A shop, café or salon with a door should show that address. A plumber, electrician or builder who visits customers should name the towns they actually cover. Leave off a town you will not travel to.
4. **The hours.** If the listing says Tuesday 9 to 5 and the site says closed on Tuesday, people trust neither. A bank holiday or a lunch break belongs in both places.
5. **What you do.** Services on the listing should be jobs you actually take. The pages should use the same words a customer would type.
6. **The website link.** It should open your site, on the secure address, and not a page you retired last year.

If one line is wrong, correct that line. Do not add a second phone number just for the listing.

## The Google Business Profile website link

The Google Business Profile website link is what people tap. Check four things.

- It uses https, so the browser does not warn that the connection is not secure.
- It opens the homepage, or a page that matches the listing, rather than an old offer.
- It can be read on a phone without pinching. If people have to zoom to find the number, start with [what to do if your website is not mobile friendly](/blog/website-not-mobile-friendly/).
- It is your site. A Facebook or Instagram page can sit beside a website. It is a poor stand-in once you have a domain of your own.

## Hours, services and the pages

Write the hours in the same form in both places. “9–5” on the listing and “open until late” on the site is not a match. If you close for a week, change both before you go.

Name the jobs the way a customer says them, and only the jobs you do. A plumber’s pages might say boiler repair, leaks and bathrooms. An electrician’s pages might say fuse boards, landlord certificates and EV chargers. A café needs the menu as real text, not only a photograph of a chalkboard. Guides for those three are [websites for plumbers](/websites-for/plumbers/), [websites for electricians](/websites-for/electricians/) and [websites for cafés](/websites-for/cafes/). The wider list is [websites by type of business](/websites-for/).

What belongs on the site itself is in [what a small business website needs](/blog/what-a-small-business-website-needs/). The listing is the short version: who you are, what you do, where, when, and the link.

## Photos, the wording, and who owns the listing

Use photos you took of the premises, the van or finished work. Ask before a customer’s home or face is in the frame. I will not put up stock photos of someone else’s job and call them yours.

The description is a few plain sentences: what you do, where, and how to start. The same facts should be readable on the website as text, not only inside a logo. That also makes the business easier for a person, or a tool such as ChatGPT, to describe without guessing. [Whether ChatGPT will recommend a business](/blog/will-chatgpt-recommend-my-business/) is a separate note. Nothing here means you will be mentioned.

The practical task is to link your website to your Google Business Profile, then match your website and your Google listing on the name, phone, address or area, and hours. Your Google Business Profile and your website can both look finished and still disagree.

You stay the Google owner. If I work on the listing, I am added as a manager, and you can remove that access. I will not ask for the Google account password, write a review, or ask anyone to post one you did not give. If a profile is suspended or stuck in verification, I can say what Google is asking for. ## A short check before you enquire

1. Search your business name on your phone and read the name, phone, hours and address or area.
2. Tap the website link. It should open your site, with the padlock, and without a warning.
3. Read those same facts on the site. Where they are facts, they should match.
4. If only the link is missing, say so. If several facts disagree, say that too, so the quote matches the job.

## Quick answers

**Do I need a website as well as a Google Business Profile?**
No. A profile can exist on its own. A working website link gives people a place to check you before they ring. If you have both, the name, phone, hours and area should match.

**Will a tidy listing put the business at the top of Maps?**
No. I can’t promise that, and nobody honestly can. The work is a complete profile that agrees with the site.

**Who should own the Google Business Profile?**
You. A designer can be a manager. They do not need your Google password, and they should not own the profile.
