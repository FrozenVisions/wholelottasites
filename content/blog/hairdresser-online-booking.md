Hairdresser online booking is a client asking for a cut, a colour or a beard trim from your site. A regular job can be booked there. A big colour change, or extensions, usually needs you to see the hair first. I’m Chris Faulkes, a web designer in Mansfield. I don’t run a salon, and I don’t sell a booking product. Cuts and barbering are on [hairdresser website design](/websites-for/hairdressers/). Nails and skin are on [beauty salon websites](/websites-for/salons/). The general button, form or phone is in [booking from a salon website](/blog/salon-booking-website/). This note is the hair and barber part.

## What can be booked, and what needs a chat

Book online when the job, the time and the price are settled: a regular cut, a fringe trim, a blow-dry, a men’s cut or beard trim at a set price, or a root retouch on a colour you already hold for that client.

Ask first when you need to see the hair:

- A big colour change, going much lighter, or correcting another salon’s colour.
- Extensions.
- A restyle where length and thickness change the time.
- Bridal or occasion hair, if the style is not agreed.
- Any job the booking system cannot price.

If every new client starts with a consultation, say that once, at the top of the list.

## Colour and a patch test

Beside each colour, say whether you want a patch test and whether it is a separate visit. Write the number of days you already ask for. If the colour is not held until the test is done, say so before they pick a time. Use the words you use in the salon. I’m not a doctor or a solicitor, so I will not set that gap or write a medical warning.

## Durations and “from” prices

Put the time in text beside the service, using your own figures. “Cut and finish: allow [your minutes].” “Restyle: allow from [your shortest time]. Longer hair takes longer.” “Colour: allow from [your shortest colour], after we have agreed it.”

“From” on a time is the shortest sitting you still do. “From” on a price is the lowest price you still charge. Name what moves it: length, thickness, or which stylist. Write both prices when short and long hair differ. Don’t publish a “from” price you never charge. Keep the list as text, not a photo of the board. A photo is awkward on a phone, and one price change needs a new picture. The page and the booking system should show the same figure.

## Deposits and cancellations

Show any deposit before the button. You choose the amount, when it is taken, and what a late cancellation does. “We take a deposit of [your amount] when this appointment is booked. Cancel with less than [your notice] and [say if the deposit is kept or returned].” A colour or extensions can take a deposit when a cut does not. Say if a cut has none. I’m not a solicitor. This is a shape for the rule you already use, not legal wording, and not advice on what you may keep.

## Walk-ins at a barber’s

“Walk-ins welcome” only fits the hours you take them. If Saturday is full and walk-ins are weekday mornings, write that. If you take none, write “book ahead”. A booked time holds a chair. A walk-in waits. Don’t promise an empty queue. Name who is bookable online and who is walk-in only, or “any barber” will reserve the wrong person.

## The book button on a phone

People open the price list on a phone. Put “book” or “request a time” at the top and again under the list, not only in the menu. A button fixed to the bottom is fine if it doesn’t cover the prices. If the page has to be pinched to read, see [what to do if your website is not mobile friendly](/blog/website-not-mobile-friendly/).

The button opens the system you already pay for, or a form if you have none. I link that system. I don’t sell one, and I won’t describe another company’s product. An ask-first service must not be an instant slot. Say that a form does not hold the time, and keep the phone number as text. Use the same name, address, phone and hours on your Google Business Profile. I can’t promise where the listing appears.
