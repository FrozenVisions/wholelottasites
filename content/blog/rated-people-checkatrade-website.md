## What to publish on it

Keep a directory membership if the work is worth it and you accept the terms they explain. I don’t sell Checkatrade memberships, and I can’t see your leads. The site beside it needs six things.

1. **The jobs, in the customer’s words.** Boiler repair, a consumer-unit change, a kitchen extension, a redecoration: only the work you do. See [what to put on a plumber’s website](/blog/plumber-website-checklist/) and [a website for a roofer](/blog/website-for-a-roofer/).
2. **The towns, written out.**
3. **A phone number in real text**, large enough to tap, with the hours beside it. A number inside a photo of the van cannot be dialled. A missed call is covered in [a missed-call text-back for trades](/blog/missed-call-text-back-for-trades/).
4. **A short form** for work that can wait: name, phone or email, town or postcode, and the job.
5. **Photos of your own work**, with permission. I will not use a stock photo of someone else’s van.
6. **The same name, number and area** on your [Google Business Profile](/google-business-profile/) and on the site, with the website link filled in. The comparison is in [your Google Business Profile and your website](/blog/google-business-profile-and-website/).

Link to reviews you already have. Leave a star rating off the page, and copy a directory review across only when that customer has agreed. The Rated People page says existing reviews stay where they are. Name Gas Safe, NICEIC, NAPIT or insurance only when you hold it.
