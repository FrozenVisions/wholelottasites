A salon booking website is the page a client opens when they want a cut, a colour, nails or another treatment, and a time. They need the service, a price or a “from” price, how long to allow, and a way to ask for the slot. I’m Chris Faulkes, a web designer. This is for the owner. I don’t run a salon.

Cuts, colour and barbering are on [hairdresser website design](/websites-for/hairdressers/). ## What booking from the site looks like

A button that says “book” or “request a time” sits on the home page, beside the price list, and on the contact page. It opens the booking system you already pay for, a form on your own site, or a phone number and a WhatsApp chat you answer.

Write the service, the price and the duration as text next to the button, not as a photo of the price card. I link the system you already use. I don’t sell a separate booking product. If it has a public page, the button can open it, with your prices still readable on your own site.

## Three ways to take the request

**Link the system you already use.** Point the button at the app or page clients already book through, and show the same services, prices and staff names. When the two copies disagree, the client cannot tell which to trust. If a stylist, a barber or a treatment can only be booked by asking you, say so beside the button.

**A simple enquiry or booking request form.** Ask for a name, a phone number or email, the service, a preferred day and time, and a short note. Say that you will confirm. A form is a request, not a held appointment, so write that above the button and keep the number there too. Send the form to yourself before you publish it.

**Phone or WhatsApp.** Put the number in real text, large enough to tap. Add WhatsApp only if you read it during published hours, and ask for the service, the day, and whether they have visited before. Say if a day off is closed for bookings.

Say beside the button if a colour correction, a party or a patch test stays on the phone while a standard appointment uses the system.

## What to put next to the booking button

- **Services.** Cuts, colour, barbering, nails, brows, and the rest of what you still do.
- **Prices.** The price you charge, or a “from” price where hair length or the work changes it.
- **Durations.** How long to allow. If a colour needs a patch test on another day, say so.
- **Deposits and cancellations.** If you take a deposit, give the amount and when it is due, before anyone requests a time. Put the cancellation rule you already use beside it.
- **Opening hours.** Days, late nights, closed days, bank holidays you know, and any time same-day bookings stop.
- **Where you are.** The address, which door, parking, and any step. List the towns if you travel, or how to arrive if you work from home.

Name the person when clients book a stylist, a barber or a therapist. Use daylight photos of your own work and room, and ask before a client’s face is shown. Don’t copy another salon’s photos, and don’t borrow reviews. When you have Google reviews, link to that profile.

## On a phone, and on Google

The price list should read on a phone without pinching to zoom, and the booking button should still be easy to find after it. If the current site fails that, see [make my website mobile friendly](/mobile-friendly-website/). Serve the pages over HTTPS. “Home” is a weak title. “Book a haircut in [the town]” is fair when that is the page and the town is yours.

Use the same name, address, phone and hours on your Google Business Profile. If the profile has a space for an appointment or booking link, point it at the same place as the button: your booking page, or the [contact](/contact/) page for a form or a call. The link should open a page that still shows the prices, the durations and the cancellation rule. I can’t promise where the listing appears.
