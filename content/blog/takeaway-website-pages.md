What to put on a takeaway website is a short list. Someone in Mansfield, Nottingham or another East Midlands town is on a phone, and they want the menu, the hours, the delivery area and a way to order. I’m Chris Faulkes, a web designer in Mansfield.

A website for a takeaway is not a restaurant page with collection added. Bookings, the room and last orders sit in [what to put on a restaurant website](/blog/restaurant-website-pages/).

## What to put on a takeaway website

1. The menu as text, with the prices you charge now.
2. Collection hours, and delivery hours if they are different.
3. The places you deliver, or a clear line that you only collect.
4. A tappable phone number, your own order form, or a link to an app you already use.
5. How a customer asks about allergens before they order.
6. The same name, number, hours and address on Google.
7. Your own food photos, on a page that needs no pinching.

## The menu has to be readable

A photo of the board fails when someone is ordering from the sofa. Write the dishes, sides, drinks and prices. Change the line when a dish sells out or comes off. Use the headings from the shop, mark collection-only items, and leave old dishes off. If a special will not stay accurate, say it is on the counter. Publish a price only when you will honour it.

## Hours and the delivery area

For a takeaway website in Mansfield, Nottingham or further into the East Midlands, put collection hours on the home page and the contact page, in the same words as the window. If delivery starts later or stops earlier, write those hours apart. Add shut days and Christmas when you know them, and change a closing time on the day it changes.

Name the towns, villages or postcodes you will actually travel to. “Nearby” does not say whether the next village is in. Collection only? Say so, and give the address in text. A hatch, a side door or parking goes on the page only if it is true. Do not invent a minimum order, a delivery fee or a distance.

## How people order

Put one clear route on the home page, the menu and the contact page.

The phone number is real text, large enough to tap. That is click-to-call. A number drawn in a logo will not dial. Use the same number on the window, the bags and Google, above any form.

Your own form can ask for a name, a phone number, collection or delivery, a delivery address, the items and a note. I connect that form, or an ordering setup you already pay for. I don’t run a delivery network.

Already on a delivery app? Link to your shop and name the app, so people can see they are leaving your site. I won’t say what an app charges, and I won’t tell you to drop it. The menu, hours and number still need to live on your own site.

## Allergens, before the bag leaves

The customer is not at the counter, so a wall notice is invisible. On the menu, and again before the order is sent, say they can ask by phone or by speaking to whoever takes the order.

Add a badge only when the shop already uses it and it is right today. Recipes change, and a shared fryer can carry an ingredient the page skips. Unsure? Leave the badge off. A line on the site is not a substitute for an answer from the shop.

Official guidance is the Food Standards Agency page, [allergen guidance for food businesses](https://www.food.gov.uk/business-guidance/allergen-guidance-for-food-businesses). I opened it on 27 November 2026. That day it said the guidance will change: the UK and EU signed a sanitary and phytosanitary agreement on 20 November 2026, and rules may change. Read that page. ## Photos, Google and phones

Use daylight photos of food you sell. Ask before you show a person. No stock shots.

Your [Google Business Profile](/google-business-profile/) should share the site’s name, number, address and hours, and it should link to the site. Don’t offer delivery on the listing if the site says you don’t. [Your Google Business Profile and your website](/blog/google-business-profile-and-website/) covers the match. You keep ownership. I don’t promise a place on Google.

The menu and the order control have to work under a thumb. Use HTTPS. If the live site fails on a phone, start with [what to do if your website is not mobile friendly](/blog/website-not-mobile-friendly/).
