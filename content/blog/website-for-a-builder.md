A website for a builder has one job before the first site visit. Someone planning an extension, a loft conversion or a renovation wants to see finished work, whether you cover their town, and how to call or ask for a quote. I’m Chris Faulkes, a web designer in Mansfield, Nottinghamshire.

## What a website for a builder needs

1. **Services.** The jobs you take, in the customer’s words.
2. **Project photos.** Your own jobs, from the start to the finish, only with permission.
3. **Areas covered.** The towns you will travel to.
4. **Reviews from Google.** A link to reviews you already have. Do not invent any.
5. **A clear call or quote button.** The number in real text, and a form for jobs that can wait.
6. **A Google Business Profile** with the same name, phone number and area as the site.

“Home” is a weak title. “House extensions in [the town]” is fair when that is the work and the town.

## Name the jobs, then show the work

People type the job, not the slogan on the van. Write extensions, loft conversions, renovations, new builds and groundwork as ordinary text, and only where you offer them. Leave off roofing, electrics or decorating if they are not your trade. Say who the customer deals with, how a first visit works, and how a quote is written. If building control applies, say who deals with it.

Take pictures at the start, during the work and at the end, in daylight, and group them by job type. Ask before you show a customer’s home. If they say no, leave that job out. Phone photos in good light are enough. I crop and compress them so the page stays quick. I will not use stock photos of someone else’s site.

## The towns you cover

List the towns you will travel to. “The local area” does not say whether you will come. Skip a town you will not visit, and write the job next to the place, such as a house extension in a town you work in. See also [trades in Derby and Leicester](/blog/do-trades-in-derby-and-leicester-need-a-website/).

## Reviews, a call button, a quote form, and Google

Link to Google reviews you already have, under the same business name as the website. I don’t write reviews. If you have none yet, leave that block off. Put the number in real text on every page, large enough to tap. A number inside a logo cannot be dialled. Keep it above the form, and state the hours.

The quote form should ask for a name, a phone or email, the town or postcode, the type of work, a rough size, the timescale, and space for plans or photos. Customers can attach drawings or photos. Publish a price only when you will honour it. Most building work is priced after a look, and the page should say that.

Use the same name, phone number and area on your [Google Business Profile](/google-business-profile/) as on the website, and add the website address to the listing. You stay the owner. Setup is free with a founding-client website. On its own, setup or a fix is £149. Optional monthly management is £99–£150. That is not a promise of a place on Google or Maps.

Read the page on a phone without pinching, on HTTPS. Mention insurance or a scheme only when it is true, and do not imply a team you do not have. I will not invent years in business, a job count, an award or a review.
