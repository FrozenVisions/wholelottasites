A website for a cleaning business has a simple job. Someone is about to hand over a key, or they need an office ready for Monday, and they want to know what you clean, whether you cover their street, and how to book or ask for a quote. I’m Chris Faulkes, a web designer in Mansfield, Nottinghamshire. This is about the pages and the wording.

The site I build for this trade is on [websites for cleaners](/websites-for/cleaners/). The wider list is [what a small business website needs](/blog/what-a-small-business-website-needs/). ## What a website for a cleaning business needs

1. **Services.** The cleans you do, and what is included.
2. **Areas covered.** The towns you will travel to.
3. **How to book or get a quote.** A form for jobs that can wait, and a phone number for jobs that cannot.
4. **Reviews.** Link to Google reviews once you have them. Do not invent any.
5. **Contact, with click-to-call.** The number in real text, on every page, large enough to tap.
6. **A quote form.** The details you need before you can price the job.

A home page can hold the short version. “Home” is a weak title. “End of tenancy cleaning in [the town]” is fair when that is the work and the town.

## Name the cleans in the customer’s words

People type the job, not the slogan on the van. Write regular house clean, deep clean, end of tenancy clean, after-builders clean, oven clean and office cleaning as ordinary text, and only where you offer them.

Split homes and offices if you do both. Say what is included. Ovens, inside windows, fridges and ironing are the lines people argue about. Say whether you bring products, and how often a regular clean can be. If a round is full, say so. Publish a price only when you will honour it.

## The towns you cover, and the phrases people type

List the towns you will travel to, with your hours. “The local area” does not say whether you will come. If you stop at a distance, say so.

Give the main towns a short page, or a clear section. Skip a town you will not visit. Write the service in a normal sentence next to the place: an end of tenancy clean in a town you cover, office cleaning where you go, a regular house clean on your round. That is the local SEO. I don’t promise a Google position or a number of leads.

## Google reviews, a tappable number, and the quote form

Use the same name, phone number and area on your Google Business Profile as on the website, and add the website address to the listing. You stay the owner. Setup is free with a founding-client website. See [Google Business Profile](/google-business-profile/). Link the profile when you have Google reviews. I don’t write reviews.

Put the number in real text on every page, large enough to tap. That is the click-to-call. A number inside a logo cannot be dialled. Keep it above the form.

Ask for a name, a phone or email, the town or postcode, the type of clean and the size. Bedrooms and bathrooms are enough for a home. For an office, ask how big it is and how often. Add pets, preferred days, and how keys or access work. If you would rather not publish a home address, say that you visit the customer.

## Phones first, and only what is true

These searches usually happen on a phone. The page should read without pinching to zoom, the number should be easy to tap, and the site should use HTTPS. Use photos of your own work only with permission.

State a trust line only when it is true.

- **Insurance.** Mention public liability insurance if you hold it. If you do not, leave it off.
- **A DBS check.** Mention one only for a person who has it, in the words on the certificate. Do not say all staff are checked unless that is true. A basic check is not an enhanced check.
- **Who turns up, keys and products.** Say who attends, how a key is kept, and whether you bring products.

I will not invent years in business, a number of homes, an award or a review. Services, the area and a phone number are enough to launch.
