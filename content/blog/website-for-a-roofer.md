A website for a roofer has one job before the first ladder goes up. A customer wants to see finished work, whether you cover their town, and how to call or ask for a quote. I’m Chris Faulkes, a web designer in Mansfield, Nottinghamshire.

## What a website for a roofer needs

1. **Services.** The jobs you take, in the customer’s words.
2. **Photos of real jobs.** Your own work, from the start to the finish, only with permission.
3. **Service area.** The towns you will travel to.
4. **Reviews from Google.** A link to reviews you already have. Ask real customers. Do not invent any.
5. **Click-to-call.** The number in real text, large enough to tap, on every page.
6. **A quote form.** For jobs that can wait until you are off the roof.
7. **A Google Business Profile** with the same name, phone number and area as the site.

“Roof repairs in [the town]” is a fair page title when that is the work and the town.

## Name the jobs, then show the work

People type the job, not the slogan on the van. Roof repairs, flat roofs, guttering and chimneys are examples of lines roofers commonly list. Write them as ordinary text, and only where you offer them. Say who the customer deals with, and how a quote is written.

Take daylight photos of your own jobs and group them by job type. Ask before you show a customer’s home. I will not use stock photos of someone else’s roof.

## The towns you cover

List the towns you will travel to. “The local area” does not say whether you will come. Skip a town you will not visit, and write the job next to the place, such as a roof repair in a town you work in. See also [trades in Derby and Leicester](/blog/do-trades-in-derby-and-leicester-need-a-website/).

## Reviews, a call button, a quote form, and Google

Link to Google reviews you already have, under the same business name as the website. I don’t write reviews. Ask the customer who had the work done, after the job, and only if they are willing. A founding-client website includes a review starter pack: a QR code, plus review-request text and email scripts. Digital only. If you have none yet, leave that block off.

Put the number in real text on every page, large enough to tap. That is the click-to-call. A number inside a logo cannot be dialled. Keep it above the form, and state the hours. If you miss the call, see [missed-call text-back for trades](/blog/missed-call-text-back-for-trades/).

The quote form should ask for a name, a phone or email, the town or postcode, the type of work, the timescale, and photos of the roof, the gutter or the ceiling. Publish a price only when you will honour it.

Use the same name, phone number and area on your [Google Business Profile](/google-business-profile/) as on the website, and add the website address to the listing. You stay the owner. Setup is free with a founding-client website. On its own, setup or a fix is £149. Optional monthly management is £99–£150. That is not a promise of a place on Google or Maps. Mention insurance or an accreditation only when you hold it. Serve the page on HTTPS, and read it on a phone without pinching.
