When a website looks outdated, people notice before they read the services. The phone number can be right and the hours can be right, and the page can still feel years behind. That is a different job from correcting a wrong price. I’m Chris Faulkes, a web designer in Mansfield. The signs are below, then three ways to act: a quick fix, a rescue, or a rebuild. Changing facts that have gone stale is covered in [how to update an old website](/blog/update-my-old-website/).

## Signs your website is out of date

An old-fashioned website usually shows on the first screen. Check it on a phone and on a laptop.

- “Welcome to our website”, with no trade and no town.
- Tiny or pale type, a letter-style font, or headlines in heavy shadow boxes.
- Glossy or bevelled buttons, or a button that only says “Click here”.
- A banner that slides on its own, with words that are hard to read.
- Stock handshakes or clip-art, rather than photos of the work.
- Music that starts on its own, a visitor counter, or a page that asks people to enter before they see the business.
- A copyright year that is not this year, or a badge for a scheme you are not in.
- A fixed-width page, a hover-only menu, or a phone number drawn inside a logo. If the phone is the problem, see [what to do if your website is not mobile friendly](/blog/website-not-mobile-friendly/).

One item can be a tidy-up. Several together are why someone assumes the business has gone quiet, even when the work is current.

## What to do when a website looks outdated

Sort the site into one job before you pay for a new design.

**A quick fix** is one concrete change, agreed first. The rest of the site stays.

**A rescue** keeps the pages and repairs a set of dated pieces. The words are mostly true, and you can still reach the site.

**A rebuild** is for when the dated look is the build itself. Small edits break other things, or the pages no longer describe the business.

An outdated website with one broken form is a mini-fix. A site that only makes sense if you already know the firm needs a rescue or a rebuild.

## A quick fix, when one thing is dated

If you can still log in, you can often change the copyright year, turn off music, remove an enter page, swap one stock photo for your own work (with permission), or retype the phone number as text, when that really is the only fault.

## Checklist before you pay

1. Open the home page on your own phone. Say what the business does, and where, without zooming.
2. Mark every sign above. One item points to a fix. A page full of them points to a rescue or a rebuild.
3. Correct anything simply untrue: the number, the hours, a price, a service you have stopped.
4. Choose the job: the £50 + VAT mini-fix, a rescue after a free audit, or the founding-price rebuild at £200 + VAT.
5. Keep the domain. Point it at a new site only when that site is ready.
