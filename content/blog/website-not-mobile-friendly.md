If your website is not mobile friendly, a customer on a phone has to pinch, zoom or scroll sideways to read it, or cannot tap the number to call you. I’m Chris Faulkes, a web designer in Mansfield, in the East Midlands. The checks below find the fault. I can’t promise a number of enquiries, or a position on Google.

A rebuild is covered on [make my website mobile friendly](/mobile-friendly-website/). Do the checks first.

## Open it on your own phone

A narrow desktop window is not the test, and a builder preview can hide a menu that only works with a mouse. Use your own phone and write down what fails.

1. Read the first screen aloud. Say what the business does, and where, without zooming.
2. Scroll. Sideways movement means a picture, a table, a map or a row of buttons is wider than the screen.
3. Tap the phone number. If nothing happens, it sits inside a logo or a photo and needs to be real text.
4. Open the menu with a thumb. A menu that only appears on hover will not work.
5. Send the contact form to yourself. The fields and the button should be easy to use when the keyboard is open, and you should see whether it arrived.
6. Check the address bar. A warning that the connection is not secure means the site is not on HTTPS. Include that in the same job.

One failure can be a single repair. A site that fails most of the list usually needs replacing.

## Change what you can edit today

If you can still log in, do the safe edits before you pay anyone.

- Retype the phone number, email and address as text, large enough to tap, and use that same number on invoices and your Google Business Profile.
- Replace a photo of a price list, menu or timetable with the words as text. A picture of a document has to be pinched. What else belongs on the pages is in [what a small business website needs](/blog/what-a-small-business-website-needs/).
- Remove a wide banner or table that forces sideways scroll, if you can do that without breaking the page. If you cannot see the cause, leave it and send the address.
- Take off hours or services that are no longer true. [How to update an old website](/blog/update-my-old-website/) is that wider tidy-up.

Keep one website. A second phone-only address means two copies of the hours and the number. A mobile plugin is unfinished until the six checks pass on a real phone. A page that shows the desktop site in miniature is usually missing the setting that tells the phone to fit the screen. That is a code change.

## Test it again

Repeat the six checks on someone else’s phone. The call should start, the form should arrive, the menu should open, and the first screen should be readable, with no sideways scroll and no security warning.

Give each page its own title. “Home” does not say what the business does. Keep the same name, phone and address on your Google Business Profile. The words still depend on the work: a consultant’s list is [what to put on a consultant’s website](/blog/consultant-website-pages/), and other businesses are under [websites for small businesses](/websites-for/).
