More people now ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Google's AI answers things like "who's a good plumber in Mansfield?" instead of scrolling a list of links. So it's a fair question: will one of these tools mention you?

The honest answer: nobody can promise it. No assistant and no search engine guarantees a mention, and anyone who says otherwise is guessing. What you can control is whether the tool can find clear, consistent facts about you when it looks.

## What an assistant needs to say your name

When an AI tool answers a local question, it pulls together what it can find and trust. That usually means:

- **Who you are.** Your business name, written the same way everywhere.
- **What you do.** Plain service names, not just "solutions". "Boiler repair" beats "heating excellence".
- **Where you work.** Your town and the areas you cover, in words on the page.
- **How to reach you.** Phone, email and opening hours that match your Google Business Profile.
- **Answers to real questions.** Prices or price ranges, call-out areas, how booking works.

If that lives only in a photo, a PDF menu or a Facebook post from 2021, the tool may get it wrong or skip you.

## Five quick checks you can do today

1. Search your business name. Do your website, Google Business Profile and any directory listings show the same name, phone number and address?
2. Open your homepage on your phone. Can you tell in five seconds what you do and where?
3. Does each main service have its own heading or page, in the words customers use?
4. Is there a short FAQ answering the questions you get on the phone every week?
5. Are your contact details written as text, not only inside an image?

If you answered no to two or more, that's the first thing to fix. It helps human visitors just as much.

## GEO is not a trick

People call this GEO, short for generative engine optimisation. It sounds technical, but most of it is ordinary good writing: clear pages, consistent facts and structured data (a bit of code that labels your name, address and services so software can read them). It's related to SEO, but the aim is being easy to quote, not chasing a position.
