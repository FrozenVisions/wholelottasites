## Wix or a custom website in Mansfield

Wix is a website builder. You pick one of their templates, change the words, photos and layout in their editor, and the published site runs on Wix. A custom site, here, is one I build for the work you do and the towns you cover. You approve the pages. You supply the words and the photos.

Wix’s own help page on [exporting or embedding your site](https://support.wix.com/en/article/exporting-or-embedding-your-wix-site-elsewhere) says three things worth knowing before you choose. The site needs to be hosted on Wix’s servers to work properly. The content you add belongs to you. Connecting your own domain means upgrading to a plan. I won’t quote a Wix price or a plan name. Those change, and the current ones are on [wix.com](https://www.wix.com). The same page describes hosting, updates and security as part of being on Wix. Read that list there if you want the detail.

## What you keep if you stay with Wix

Staying is a sensible choice when the site is finished and you will look after it.

- You can change a price, a photo or your opening hours yourself, in the editor, without emailing anyone.
- You start from a template that already has a layout, and you shape it around a straightforward business.
- Hosting is set up when you publish. You are not sorting out a separate server on the first evening.
- The words and photos you add are yours. Keep a copy of them outside the editor as well.

A finished Wix site that names the work, the towns and a phone number people can tap is a proper website. If that describes yours, keep it.

## What a Mansfield business gives up on Wix

These are the trade-offs of a hosted builder. Read them before you decide.

**Your time.** You build the pages. A template does not write your services, your towns or your prices. The work stalls when the evenings go on the job. A page titled “Home”, with the number inside a picture, does not tell a customer in Mansfield what you do or how to ring.

**The design.** You work inside Wix’s templates and editor. You can change colours, type, photos and sections. You are choosing inside that system. Businesses that stay close to the same template can look related. That is fine when a shared look does not bother you.

**Where the site lives.** The working site runs on Wix. Their help says you can’t put that same site on another host. If you later want the business somewhere else, the pages are built again and the domain is pointed at the new site. The content can come with you, because Wix says it belongs to you. The layout does not travel as a working site.

**The bill.** Connecting your own web address means upgrading to a Wix plan. Check wix.com for the current price, and for what happens if that plan ends. A custom site here is a one-off build, or 12 monthly payments for the build, and then the site is yours. From year two you pay the domain renewal and hosting, or optional monthly care. That is a different bill. The figures are below, and on [pricing](/pricing/).

**Who fixes it.** On Wix, you fix it in the editor, or someone you let into the account does. If you ask me to look at a live Wix site, I audit first. ## What you give up with a custom site

A custom site has trade-offs too. Know them before you leave a builder you are happy with.

- A wording change waits on an edit, unless you take the site on yourself or you have care for small text, photo and price changes.
- You supply the words and the photos. I won’t invent a service, a town, a registration or a review.
- You pay for the build. A Wix plan is a bill you pay to Wix.
- You wait for the pages, then you approve them. A founding client launches within 7 days of receiving the content. We tweak until you’re happy before launch.

You spend the time on the business. I spend it on the site. If you enjoy the editor, and you will keep the hours and prices current, stay with Wix.

## When a DIY builder is the sensible choice

Stay on Wix when these are true.

1. You have the hours to finish the pages, and to change them when a price moves.
2. What you do, and the towns you cover, are straightforward to write.
3. A layout built from a Wix template suits you.
4. You are content to keep a Wix plan so the site, on your own domain, stays published.
5. The domain is in your name, and you keep a copy of the words and photos.

The test is a phone. Can a stranger say what you do, where, and how to ring, without pinching to zoom? If yes, and you will keep it that way, the builder is doing the job.
